Questions

What to know before installing.

Is HikingLife free?

Yes. It is a personal project distributed directly, with no account, ads, or in-app purchases.

Does it work offline?

The app includes an offline Italy map for zoom 8–10. Before leaving you can download a country, a city, a searched place or the current view at overview (Z8–Z12) or trail detail (Z13–Z16) zoom. Other areas and higher zooms load online and are cached when a connection is available. Straight-line measuring and navigation work offline; trail routing needs a connection.

Does it track or share my location?

Recorded tracks and saved recordings stay in the app's private storage on your device. HikingLife has no account, analytics, ads, or developer-operated server, and it does not send your position to the developer. Map tiles are requested from OpenTopoMap, OSM France and Waymarked Trails, and trail distances or navigation send the start and end coordinates to the FOSSGIS/OSRM routing service. These providers receive standard network information such as your IP address.

Which Android versions are supported?

Android 8.0 (API 26) or newer.

Why is there an "unknown apps" warning?

HikingLife is installed from a browser, not Google Play, so Android asks you to allow installs from that browser. The permission applies only to the app you use to open the APK.

How do I update?

Install the new APK over the current one. The first signed release (1.2.1) is the exception: if you have the old debug build (1.2.0), uninstall it once because the signing key changed.

Are track recording and GPX available?

Track recording, live stats, saved recordings and elevation profiles are available since 1.3.0. Tracks are stored on the device in a JSON Lines format; GPX import/export is planned for a future release.

How do I report a bug?

Contact me on GitHub or LinkedIn with your Android version, device model, and steps to reproduce. Include a screenshot if possible.

Changelog

Release history, newest first.

1.16.1 · Straight buttons 30 Oct 2026
  • The menu arrow, the home button, the waypoint button and the compass button are one group, evenly spaced: a column under the menu arrow when the phone is upright, a row beside it in landscape.
  • The home button no longer touches the menu arrow, and the buttons keep the same gap when one is added or the screen changes.
1.16.0 · Follow a GPX track, leave the app, and a map that turns 30 Oct 2026
  • **Import a GPX track and follow it.** *Import GPX* in the menu opens the files of the phone: the track appears as a green line on the map and the app guides you along it from the nearest point. The panel carries the file name, the remaining distance and time and the next manoeuvre, and leaving the track only warns you — the file is the route, not a suggestion — until its end closes the walk.
  • **Export and share a recording as GPX.** Every saved recording has an actions button: *Export as GPX file* writes a standard GPX 1.1 file through the system save dialog, and *Share as GPX…* sends it to Telegram, e-mail, WhatsApp or any other app. No storage permission is asked for.
  • **Exit** at the end of the menu closes the app and removes it from the recent apps, stopping the recording service too. If a walk is still recording it asks first: *Save* keeps it, *Discard* throws the points away, *Cancel* goes back to the map.
  • **A compass button that turns the map.** By default the map keeps the direction of travel at the top, turning with the course of the walk so the path you follow stays upright; a phone lying still never turns it, and the map stays north up until a course is known. One tap makes the map turn with the phone instead, like a paper map in the hand: point the phone north and north is at the top.
  • **The position is drawn as an arrow**, not a dot: it shows where the phone points — or the GPS course on a phone without a compass — and turns with the map, so heading and map always read together.
  • The two-finger map rotation is gone: the button decides which way the map points.
1.15.0 · The home point and the waypoints 30 Oct 2026
  • The house button saves the place where you stand as home. The house stays on the map, so you always see where the walk started, and pressing the button again moves it.
  • **Return to home** in the menu starts the navigation to the home point, on the trails when they reach it and in a straight line when they do not, with the distance and the time in the panel.
  • A long press on the house button, or a tap on the house on the map, does the same.
  • The pin button asks for a nickname and saves a waypoint, which appears on the map as a blue pin.
  • **Waypoints** in the menu lists the saved places with the distance from where you are: tap one to navigate to it, and delete the ones you no longer need.
  • Home and waypoints stay on the phone and are there after a restart or an update.
  • Stopping a recording now offers **Discard** as well as Save and Cancel, so a walk can be thrown away without keeping a file.
1.14.0 · The trail navigator 29 Oct 2026
  • Long press the map and choose **Trail route**: the app works the walk out on the phone from the trails, cycleways and roads of the downloaded map. It downloads nothing and needs no connection.
  • The route is drawn in blue with the destination marked, and a small arrow on the map marks the next manoeuvre.
  • The bar at the top names the manoeuvre — continue straight, keep left, keep right, turn left or right, sharp turns, arrival — with the distance to it, and the panel below shows what is left of the walk and the time it takes.
  • Walking off the line recalculates the route from where you are, at most once every 20 s, so a wrong turn is corrected without touching the phone.
  • The straight line is still there, under **Straight line** in the same dialog, for a point that is not on a trail.
1.13.1 · The GPS keeps the ride going 29 Oct 2026
  • The GPS now counts as proof of movement on its own: from 1.5 m/s (about 5 km/h) the distance, the live speed and the moving time keep running, so a smooth ride is recorded like a hike.
  • The movement goes on for 20 s after the last fast fix and then stops, so a stop at a traffic light is not counted as travel.
  • A phone lying still still records nothing: a single jump of the position is treated as GPS drift, not as movement.
1.13.0 · The live GPS and the follow button 29 Oct 2026
  • The altitude in the status panel is live also while no recording is running: it follows the fixes the map receives and stays empty only until the GPS reports an altitude.
  • A new follow button on the map: one tap centres on your position and keeps the map on you while you walk. A second tap, or simply dragging the map, gives you the camera back.
  • The map no longer jumps back to your position a few seconds after you pan away: it moves only on the launch, when you ask for it or while follow is on.
  • The bar under the map always shows the coordinates of the latest fix, updated as you move, together with the state of the GPS signal.
  • Map colors now uses a round color wheel with a brightness slider: pick the hue and the saturation on the circle instead of moving three RGB sliders.
1.12.0 · Map colors 28 Oct 2026
  • The side menu has Map colors: pick the background, the road color and the trail color, each with a live preview and its hex value.
  • The choice applies to every downloaded map you open, is kept across launches and needs no download: the packages carry the layers, the app draws the colors.
  • Roads keep a darker edge that follows the color you choose, so they stay readable on any background, and Standard colors brings the original look back.
1.11.1 · The map after the update 28 Oct 2026
  • A map downloaded with the previous version is no longer listed as installed: the app forgets the old entry and offers the map for download again, instead of keeping a map it cannot draw.
  • When the map you selected is already on the phone but was never activated, the app activates it when it starts, so the map appears without downloading it again.
  • A map downloaded while the app still remembered an old one is now selected right away, so the map stops saying that nothing is downloaded.
  • The details of a downloaded map no longer show the word "null" when the map file has no description.
1.11.0 · Vector maps: one file for every zoom 28 Oct 2026
  • A region is now one download that stays sharp at every zoom: trails, water, woods, roads, peaks and huts are drawn on your phone instead of arriving as separate pictures for each level.
  • Peaks, huts, viewpoints, water points and place names are drawn above the map, so a name is never cut at the edge of a tile, never appears twice and never covers another name.
  • The download is smaller than the old picture pyramid for the same area, and it keeps zooming further than the old maps did.
  • Maps downloaded with the previous version are not read by this one: open the Maps menu and download your map again.
1.10.0 · One map, every zoom 28 Oct 2026
  • The map no longer stops at its deepest level: past it the app scales the highest level the file carries, so you can keep zooming in on a trail or on your position.
  • The view zooms up to four levels past the end of the map, and the picture gets softer with every level. For those levels the app never uses the network.
  • The minimum zoom is still the one of the map, so the whole area fits the screen as before. Nothing else changes in the download or in the Maps menu.
1.9.0 · One map: the one you downloaded 28 Oct 2026
  • The map of the app is now the map you download from the **Maps** menu. On a new install the map screen explains that a map is needed and opens the menu by itself; once the download finishes the map is drawn from the phone.
  • You can keep several downloaded maps and choose the one to use; the menu shows the name, zoom range, area and author of each one, and deleting a map frees the space it takes.
  • The map stops at the zoom levels your file carries, so every level you can see is really stored on the phone; the range is shown together with the map.
  • When the selected map is deleted the screen goes back to the empty state, where a single button takes you to the Maps menu.
  • Measuring and long-press navigation now always follow a straight line and work with no connection; navigation shows the remaining distance as you walk.
  • The map files still come from the public downloads repository, and a map published later appears in the menu without updating the app.
1.8.0 · The maps you want, in the Maps menu 27 Oct 2026
  • A new **Maps** entry in the side menu, next to the map style and the saved recordings. It lists the published maps, the ones you already downloaded and the one that is currently shown.
  • The published maps are read from a catalogue, so a new map shows up in the list without updating the app.
  • A map is downloaded with a progress bar, and the file is checked before it is used: a broken or incomplete download is refused instead of leaving a map that cannot be shown.
  • The map you select becomes the map of the app, and its attribution is added next to the attribution of the style, so the author of the map is always credited.
  • You can go back to the standard styles at any time. The downloaded map stays on the phone, so you can select it again later without downloading it twice.
  • The maps you no longer want can be deleted from the same menu, and the space they take is freed.
  • The address of the catalogue is shown at the top of the menu and can be changed, so you can read the maps from your own copy or from a mirror.
1.7.0 · An altitude that matches the other apps 27 Oct 2026
  • The altitude is corrected with the EGM96 geoid model. Android reports the height above the WGS-84 reference ellipsoid, while the maps and the other navigation apps show the height above mean sea level; the two differ by the geoid undulation, which is about 50 m in Europe. A spot that reads 206 m in another app now reads 206 m here too.
  • The correction applies to the recorded tracks as well, so the elevation profile of a new hike shows the real heights above sea level.
  • The altitude in the status panel is smoothed, so it no longer jumps around by a few meters with the GPS noise.
  • Tracks recorded before this version keep the old, ellipsoidal altitude: their profile sits about 50 m above the real height above sea level.
1.6.0 · Two clocks, real motion detection and a map that keeps your view 27 Oct 2026
  • Every recording has two clocks: the total time runs from the moment you start recording until you stop it, and the moving time only counts while you are really walking. A pause or a coffee break still shows on the total time, while the moving time stands still as soon as the phone lies still. Both times are saved with the track and shown in the list of saved recordings.
  • The accelerometer and the gyroscope now tell the app whether the device is moving before anything is counted. A resting phone no longer adds its GPS drift to the distance, keeps the live speed at zero and does not count phantom steps, so the numbers match what you actually walked.
  • The status panel shows the current altitude above sea level, next to the total elevation gain. The value follows the latest GPS fix, so it keeps updating even while you stand still, and stays empty when the GPS reports no altitude.
  • Stopping a recording asks for a name or a note before the track is saved. The field comes prefilled with the date and time, so a hike can be saved without typing anything; cancelling the dialog keeps the recording running. The name is shown in the saved-recordings list, and the track file is renamed to that name plus the start date, so recordings stay recognisable even outside the app.
  • Rotating the phone no longer resets the map. The place the map was showing, the zoom level and the map rotation are kept when the screen turns, instead of jumping back to the default starting view.
